SKILLPATH LEARNING NOTES

🎨 Graphic Design: Beginner's Guide

Read the notes, study the illustrations, then try the mini exercises.

Lesson 1: What is graphic design?

Graphic design is the use of text, images, colours, and shapes to communicate a message visually. You see it on posters, book covers, logos, adverts, product packaging, and social media posts.

A good design is not just beautiful. It should help people understand the message quickly.

Example: On an event poster, the event name should be easy to notice first. The date and location should also be easy to find. Decoration should never hide important information.

Try it

Think of a poster you have seen. What was its main message? Which words were largest? What picture or shape did it use?

Lesson 2: Layout and visual hierarchy

Layout is how text and images are arranged. Visual hierarchy is the order in which people notice things. Usually, the main title should stand out first, followed by supporting information, then smaller details.

Three useful rules: align related items, leave space between sections, and make the most important information stand out.

Example: For a school-club flyer, use a large title at the top, put the date and place underneath, and finish with a clear instruction such as “Join us”.

Try it

Sketch a flyer with one large title, two lines of details, and one clear action.

Lesson 3: Colour and contrast

Colour can set a mood, group related information, and attract attention. Contrast means there is enough difference between text and its background for the text to be easy to read.

Dark text on a light background, or light text on a dark background, often works well. Do not rely on colour alone to explain something; labels and symbols can help too.

Example: Black text on a pale yellow background is usually easier to read than pale yellow text on white.

Try it

Choose a background colour and a text colour for a pretend flyer. Check whether someone else can read it easily.

Lesson 4: Typography

Typography is the way text is chosen and arranged. Use readable fonts, make headings larger than body text, and avoid using too many different font styles.

Example: For a tutoring flyer, make “Tutoring Classes” the main heading, the subjects a smaller subheading, and the schedule body text.

Try it

Write a headline, a subheading, and two sentences describing an imaginary event.

Lesson 5: Images, spacing, and your first project

Images should support the message, not distract from it. Spacing is the empty area between elements; it gives the content room to breathe. Use original images or images you have permission to use.

Your project: Create a flyer for an imaginary event or small business. Include a clear title, a short description, a date or useful detail, readable colours, and one relevant picture or illustration.

Final checklist

  • Can someone understand the message in five seconds?
  • Is the text easy to read on a phone?
  • Are the date and details accurate?
  • Did you check spelling?
  • Are the images yours or permitted for use?
